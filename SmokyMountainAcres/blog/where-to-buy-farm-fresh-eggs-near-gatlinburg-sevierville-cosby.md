## What Does "Farm-Fresh Eggs" Actually Mean?

"Farm fresh" is a common phrase, but it does not automatically tell you anything specific about how a chicken was raised.

A farm-fresh egg could come from:

- a chicken living mainly indoors
- a free-range flock
- a pasture-raised flock
- hens fed conventional feed
- hens fed organic feed

That is why buying directly from a farmer can be useful.

Instead of relying entirely on a label, you can ask questions.

### Ask how the chickens are raised

Do they actually live on pasture?

Do they have outdoor access every day?

How much room do they have?

### Ask what the chickens eat

Pasture-raised and organic are not the same thing.

A pasture-raised chicken may still receive conventional feed.

At our farm, our chickens forage on pasture and receive organic feed as their supplemental ration.

### Ask how fresh the eggs are

Small farms often sell eggs much closer to the time they were laid than eggs moving through a larger commercial distribution system.

The exact timing will vary by farm, so asking is better than assuming.

## Are Local Eggs Pasture-Raised?

Not necessarily.

"Local" only tells you that the eggs were produced relatively nearby.

It does not tell you how the hens were housed.

A local farm might raise chickens:

- primarily indoors
- in movable chicken tractors
- in permanent outdoor runs
- on rotating pasture
- completely free-range
- through some combination of these systems

If pasture-raised eggs are important to you, ask the farmer directly.

## Are Farm-Fresh Eggs Organic?

Again, not necessarily.

"Farm fresh," "local," "pasture-raised," and "organic" all mean different things.

At Smoky Mountain Acres, our chickens are **pasture-raised and fed organic feed**.

That describes our farming practices.

It should not be confused with a claim that our eggs carry USDA Organic certification unless and until they are actually certified under that program.

We would rather explain exactly what we do than rely on broad marketing language.

## What Do Pasture-Raised Chickens Eat?

Pasture-raised chickens are omnivores.

When they are outside, they may eat:

- grass
- tender plants
- seeds
- beetles
- grasshoppers
- worms
- larvae
- spiders
- other insects and small invertebrates

They also generally need balanced poultry feed.

Laying eggs places significant nutritional demands on a hen, particularly for protein, energy, minerals, and calcium.

Our chickens forage naturally while also receiving **organic feed formulated for poultry**.

We think the two belong together.

Pasture gives chickens the opportunity to behave naturally.

Good feed helps make sure their nutritional needs are met.

## Why Do Egg Yolks From Local Farms Sometimes Look Darker?

People often notice that eggs from pasture-based flocks can have darker yellow or orange yolks.

Yolk color is strongly influenced by what chickens eat.

Access to green plants, insects, and various feed ingredients can change yolk pigmentation.

However, yolk color by itself does not prove that an egg is pasture-raised, organic, or nutritionally superior.

Some commercial feed formulations can also produce darker yolks.

If you want to know how a chicken was raised, it is better to ask the farmer than to judge the farming system by yolk color.

## Are Brown Eggs Better Than White Eggs?

No.

Shell color mainly comes down to genetics.

Different chicken breeds lay different colored eggs.

Depending on the flock, you may see:

- white
- cream
- light brown
- dark brown
- blue
- green
- olive-colored eggs

Shell color does not tell you whether the bird was pasture-raised or fed organic feed.

A colorful carton can be enjoyable, but the shell is not a nutritional grading system.

## What Should You Ask Before Buying Local Eggs?

If you are looking for eggs near Gatlinburg, Sevierville, Cosby, or elsewhere in East Tennessee, these questions will tell you far more than the phrase "farm fresh."

### Are your chickens pasture-raised?

Ask whether the birds genuinely spend their time foraging outdoors.

### What do you feed your chickens?

Ask specifically whether the feed is conventional, non-GMO, organic, or certified organic.

### Do you use pesticides or herbicides where they forage?

This matters if avoiding those inputs is important to your household.

### How often are eggs collected?

Small farms may collect once or several times per day.

### When were these eggs laid?

This gives you a much better idea of freshness than simply seeing "farm fresh" written on a sign.

### Do you sell directly from the farm?

Some farms use self-service stands, scheduled pickup, farmers markets, or appointment-based sales.

Always check before arriving.

## Are Eggs Available Year-Round?

Egg availability can change throughout the year.

Spring and summer typically bring longer daylight hours and stronger production, while hens often slow down as daylight decreases.
